Pressure and Fluid Dynamics

Why do snowshoes work, why are dams thicker at the bottom, how does a tiny push lift a car, why does a steel ship float — and how does a 400-tonne aeroplane stay in the sky? Every tab has a story, a hands-on lab and a mission.

Notes

All the physics of this unit, with worked examples. Use them alongside the labs.

Pressure in solids

The same force can sink you or hold you up — it depends on the area.

Pressure in liquids

Deeper means more pressure — in every direction.

Atmospheric pressure

We live at the bottom of an ocean of air.

Gas pressure

Billions of tiny collisions: the particle model of gas pressure, Boyle’s law and the pressure law.

Hydraulics

Pascal’s principle: a small force on a small piston becomes a large force on a large one.

Floating and sinking

Archimedes’ principle: upthrust equals the weight of the fluid pushed aside.

Fluids in motion

Continuity, viscosity, laminar and turbulent flow, and drag.

Bernoulli and lift

Where a fluid moves faster, its pressure is lower — and wings use this.
