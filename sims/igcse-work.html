01What is work?

In physics, work has a precise meaning. Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance in the direction of the force.

Key ideaMechanical or electrical work done is equal to the energy transferred.
work done = energy transferred

So doing work is simply one of the ways energy moves from one store to another. If you do 500 J of work lifting a box, 500 J of energy has been transferred, from the chemical store of your muscles to the gravitational potential store of the box.

02The equation for mechanical work

W = F d = ΔE
W
work done, in joules (J)
F
force, in newtons (N)
d
distance moved in the direction of the force, in metres (m)
ΔE
energy transferred, in joules (J)
Units1 joule is the work done when a force of 1 newton moves an object 1 metre in the direction of the force. So 1 J = 1 N m.
Rearranged forms
F = frac{W}{d}
d = frac{W}{F}

04When is NO work done?

A force does no work if:

  • The object does not move. Pushing hard against a wall, or holding a heavy bag still, does no work on the wall or the bag. (You get tired because your muscles are working internally, but no energy is transferred to the object.)
  • The object moves at right angles to the force. A waiter carrying a tray across a room pushes up on it, but it moves sideways, so the upward force does no work on the tray. Earth’s gravity on a satellite in a circular orbit does no work either, because the force always points at right angles to the motion.
  • There is no force. A puck sliding at constant speed on frictionless ice has no force acting along its motion, so no work is done on it.
Common mistake“Work” in physics is not the same as effort. You must have both a force and movement in the direction of that force.

05Work and energy stores

What the work does depends on what the force is working against:

Work done…Energy goes to…Example
against gravity (lifting)gravitational potential storeA crane lifting a girder
to speed something upkinetic storeA footballer kicking a ball
against friction or air resistancethermal store of the surroundingsPushing a box at constant speed, braking
stretching or squashingelastic storeDrawing a bow, compressing a spring

When lifting at a steady speed, the force needed equals the weight: F = mg. So the work done against gravity is:

W = F d = (m g) × h = m g h = ΔE_p

This is exactly the change in GPE from topic 1.7.1. The two topics are the same idea seen from two sides.

BrakingTo stop a moving car, the brakes must do work equal to its kinetic energy: F × d = ½mv². So the braking distance is d = ½mv² ÷ F. Doubling the speed makes the braking distance four times longer.

06Worked examples

Example 6.1A student pushes a box with a force of 50 N across a floor for 4.0 m. Calculate the work done.
W = F d = 50 × 4.0 => 200 J # So 200 J of energy has been transferred.
Example 6.2A 20 kg box is lifted 1.5 m at a steady speed. Calculate the work done.
# The lifting force equals the weight: F = m g = 20 × 9.8 = 196 N W = F d = 196 × 1.5 => 294 J # This equals the gain in GPE.
Example 6.3A tractor does 1500 J of work pulling a log with a force of 300 N. Calculate how far the log moves.
d = frac{W}{F} = frac{1500}{300} => 5.0 m
Example 6.4A cyclist does 2.4 kJ of work pedalling 80 m. Calculate the average forward force.
# Convert: 2.4 kJ = 2400 J F = frac{W}{d} = frac{2400}{80} => 30 N
Example 6.5A 1000 kg car travelling at 20 m/s brakes with a constant force of 8000 N. Calculate the braking distance.
# Step 1: kinetic energy to remove E_k = frac{1}{2} × 1000 × 20^2 = 200 000 J # Step 2: work done by the brakes = energy transferred F d = 200 000 d = frac{200 000}{8000} => 25 m
Example 6.6A box is pushed at constant speed with a force of 120 N for 15 m. Calculate the energy transferred to the thermal store.
W = 120 × 15 => 1800 J # Constant speed means the kinetic energy does not change, so all 1800 J of work is done against friction and goes to the thermal store.
Example 6.7A motor is supplied with 600 J by electrical work. It lifts a 30 kg load through 1.5 m. Calculate the energy wasted.
mechanical work = m g h = 30 × 9.8 × 1.5 = 441 J wasted = 600 − 441 => 159 J

07Force–distance graphs

If the force changes as the object moves, the work done is the area under the force–distance graph. For a constant force the area is a rectangle (F × d). For a force that rises steadily from zero, as when stretching a spring, the area is a triangle:

W = frac{1}{2} × F_{max} × d

08Exam technique

Calculation checklist1. Write W = Fd. 2. Make sure d is the distance in the direction of the force (for lifting, use the vertical height, not the slope length). 3. Convert kN → N and km → m. 4. Give the unit: J (or N m).
Watch outFor lifting questions you are often given a mass. Convert it to a force first: weight = mg.

Electrical work vs mechanical work

What is happening?

The power supply drives a current through the motor. The current does electrical work: this is the energy transferred to the motor. The motor then does mechanical work on the load, lifting it and filling its GPE store. A motor is never 100% efficient, so some of the electrical work is wasted heating the motor and as sound.
