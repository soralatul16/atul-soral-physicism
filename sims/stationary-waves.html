8.1 · Waves that add up

Stationary Waves

When two waves meet, their displacements simply add — the principle of superposition. When a wave meets its own reflection, that addition locks into a fixed pattern of nodes and antinodes: a stationary wave, which stores energy rather than transferring it.

AS · Standard

The principle of superposition: when two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the vector sum of their individual displacements. Change the phase difference between two equal waves and watch the resultant swing between reinforcement and cancellation.

Add the displacements

Faint blue and cyan curves are the two component waves; the bold curve is their sum. At 0° they reinforce (constructive); at 180° they cancel (destructive).

The resultant

y = y₁ + y₂
Two waves of equal amplitude 3.0 cm meet at a point. (The resultant amplitude of two equal waves is 2A cos(φ/2), where φ is the phase difference.)
Question
(a) State the resultant amplitude when the waves arrive in phase (φ = 0). [1]
6.0 cm — the two displacements always add, giving constructive superposition (2 × 3.0 cm).
(b) State the resultant amplitude when the waves are in antiphase (φ = 180°). [1]
0 — equal and opposite displacements cancel at every instant (destructive superposition).
(c) Calculate the resultant amplitude when φ = 90°. [2]
2A cos(φ/2) = 2 × 3.0 × cos45°=6.0 × 0.707≈4.2 cm
AS · Standard

Send a wave along a string and reflect it back: the incident and reflected waves (same speed, frequency and amplitude, opposite directions) superpose. At some points they always cancel (nodes); midway between, they always reinforce (antinodes). The pattern no longer travels — it is a stationary wave.

Two waves build a standing pattern

Blue = incident (travelling right), cyan = reflected (travelling left), bold violet = their sum. Red dots are nodes (always still); orange dots are antinodes (maximum swing).

Nodes, antinodes & spacing

node → node = λ2,  node → antinode = λ4

Derivation · where the nodes come from

Superpose an incident and a reflected wave of equal amplitude. Reveal one line at a time.

Step 1 · the two waves
y₁ = A sin(kx − ωt),  y₂ = A sin(kx + ωt)
Same amplitude and frequency, travelling in opposite directions.
Step 2 · add them
y = y₁ + y₂ = 2A sin(kx) cos(ωt)
Using the sum-to-product identity. The x and t parts have separated.
Step 3 · nodes
sin(kx) = 0 ⇒ x = 0, λ2, λ, …
Where sin(kx)=0 the displacement is always zero — a node. Nodes are λ/2 apart.
Step 4 · antinodes
|sin(kx)| = 1 ⇒ amplitude = 2A
Midway between nodes the amplitude is greatest (2A) — an antinode. The cos(ωt) term just sets every point oscillating in step.
A stationary wave is set up on a string. Adjacent nodes are found to be 0.40 m apart.
Question
(a) State the wavelength of the waves forming the stationary wave. [1]
Node spacing = λ/2, so λ = 2 × 0.40 = 0.80 m.
(b) State the distance from a node to the nearest antinode. [1]
λ/4 = 0.20 m.
(c) State two differences between this stationary wave and a progressive wave. [2]
A stationary wave stores energy (no net transfer along it) whereas a progressive wave transfers energy; and all points between adjacent nodes oscillate in phase with different amplitudes, whereas in a progressive wave every point has the same amplitude but a steadily changing phase.
AS · Standard

Three standard experiments produce stationary waves. A stretched string vibrates between two fixed ends; an air column resonates in a pipe; microwaves form a standing pattern between a transmitter and a reflector. Pick one and step through its modes.

String fixed at both ends

Both ends are fixed, so each is a node. The nth harmonic fits n half-wavelengths into the length L.

Harmonics on the string

L = nλ2⇒λ = 2Ln

Air column in a pipe

A closed end forces a displacement node; an open end is a displacement antinode (end corrections ignored).

Resonant modes

Microwaves and a metal reflector

Microwaves from the transmitter reflect off the metal sheet; the two waves superpose into a stationary wave. A probe receiver finds minima (nodes) half a wavelength apart.

Finding the wavelength

λ = 2 × (node spacing)
A string of length 1.2 m is fixed at both ends and vibrates in its third harmonic (n = 3). The wave speed on the string is 72 m s⁻¹.
Question
(a) Calculate the wavelength of the stationary wave. [2]
λ = 2Ln = 2 × 1.23=0.80 m
(b) Calculate the frequency of vibration. [2]
f = vλ = 720.80=90 Hz
(c) State the number of nodes and antinodes on the string in this mode. [2]
Third harmonic: 4 nodes (including the two fixed ends) and 3 antinodes.
AS · Standard

Four problems across superposition, node spacing and resonant modes. Try each before revealing the solution.

Worked Example · Superposition
Two waves of equal amplitude 2.0 cm arrive at a point with a phase difference of 60°. Find the resultant amplitude. (Resultant = 2A cos(φ/2).)
2A cos(φ/2) = 2 × 2.0 × cos30°=4.0 × 0.866≈3.5 cm

Between full reinforcement (4.0 cm) and complete cancellation (0).

Worked Example · Node spacing to wavelength
On a stationary wave, five successive nodes span a distance of 60 cm. Find the wavelength.

Five nodes enclose four node-spacings, each λ/2.

4 × λ2 = 60 cm⇒λ = 2 × 604=30 cm
Worked Example · String harmonics
A string of length 0.60 m fixed at both ends supports waves of speed 120 m s⁻¹. Find the fundamental frequency and the frequency of the second harmonic.

Fundamental (n = 1): λ = 2L = 1.2 m.

f₁ = vλ = 1201.2=100 Hz

The second harmonic has twice the fundamental frequency:

f₂ = 2f₁=200 Hz
Worked Example · Closed pipe
A pipe closed at one end is 0.17 m long. Taking the speed of sound as 340 m s⁻¹, find the fundamental frequency.

Closed pipe fundamental: node at the closed end, antinode at the open end, so L = λ/4.

λ = 4L = 0.68 m⇒f = vλ = 3400.68=500 Hz
AS · Standard

The traps on stationary waves. Each card pairs the wrong idea with the correct physics.

"A stationary wave transfers energy along its length."

There is no net transfer of energy along a stationary wave. Energy stays stored, sloshing between kinetic and potential within each loop.

No net energy transfer along it.
"Every point on a stationary wave has the same amplitude."

Amplitude varies with position: zero at the nodes, maximum (2A) at the antinodes, and in between elsewhere.

Amplitude ranges from 0 (nodes) to 2A (antinodes).
"Adjacent nodes are one wavelength apart."

Adjacent nodes are half a wavelength apart; a node and its neighbouring antinode are a quarter wavelength apart.

node → node = λ/2.
"Points between two nodes are out of phase."

All points in the same loop (between adjacent nodes) oscillate in phase — reaching their extremes together. The phase flips by π only as you cross a node.

Same loop → in phase; across a node → antiphase.
"A closed end of a pipe is a displacement antinode."

Air cannot move at a closed end, so it is a displacement node. The open end is the antinode.

Closed end = node; open end = antinode.
"You need two separate sources to make a stationary wave."

Usually a single wave and its own reflection are enough — same frequency and amplitude, travelling in opposite directions.

One wave + its reflection will do.
