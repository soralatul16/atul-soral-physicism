Let’s Explore Radiations: Atomic Physics

What is inside an atom, and how did we find out? Why do some nuclei break apart? What are alpha, beta and gamma radiation, how do we detect them, and how can something so dangerous also save lives and power cities? Every tab has a story, a hands-on lab and a mission. ★ Extension boxes go further for MYP 5.

Key concept: Change  ·  Related concepts: models, evidence, energy, consequences, balance  ·  Global context: Scientific and technical innovation
Statement of inquiry: Evidence about how unstable nuclei change has transformed our models of the atom, and lets us use radiation responsibly — weighing its benefits against its risks to people and the environment.

Notes

Models of the atom

How evidence changed our picture of the atom — and Rutherford’s experiment that found the nucleus.

Build an atom

Why some nuclei last for ever and others break apart — and how to predict how they will decay.

Detecting radiation

The Geiger–Müller tube, background radiation and corrected count rates.

Decay equations

Half-life

Radioactive decay is random — but the half-life is predictable. Read half-life graphs and date ancient objects.

Fission and fusion

Splitting heavy nuclei and joining light ones: chain reactions, control and the power of the stars.

Nuclear power plant

Run a reactor: from nuclear energy to electricity, safely.

Radiation and living things

How ionising radiation damages cells, how dose is measured, and how people work safely.

Uses of radiation

Medicine, industry, food, smoke alarms and dating: choosing the right isotope for the job.
