Question
An oil drop of weight 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ N hangs stationary between plates 1.0 cm apart with 500 V across them.
(a) State the relationship between the forces on the drop at balance. [1]
The upward electric force equals the downward weight: qE = mg, i.e. qV/d = mg.
(b) Calculate the charge on the drop. [2]
q = mgdV=(3.2 × 10⁻¹⁴)(0.010)500=6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
(c) How many excess electrons does the drop carry? [2]
n = qe=6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹=4 electrons
A whole number — evidence that charge is quantized.

The usual traps on electric fields. Each card pairs the wrong idea with the correct physics.

"A bigger test charge sits in a stronger field."

E = F/q is independent of the test charge. A bigger q feels a bigger force, but the ratio — the field strength — is the same.

E depends on the source and position only.
"Field lines are real, solid lines."

They are a drawing tool. Their direction shows the force on a positive charge and their spacing shows the strength.

Lines are a model, not objects.
"The field inside a charged metal sphere is strong."

Inside a conductor the field is zero; all the charge sits on the outer surface.

E = 0 inside a conductor.
"Between parallel plates the field is stronger near a plate."

The field is uniform — same strength and direction everywhere between the plates (apart from the edges).

Uniform field: E = V/d throughout.
"N C⁻¹ and V m⁻¹ are different quantities."

They are the same unit for field strength. E = F/q gives N C⁻¹; E = V/d gives V m⁻¹; they are identical.

N C⁻¹ = V m⁻¹.
"Field lines can cross where two charges meet."

The resultant field has one direction at each point, so lines never cross — they bend around instead.

Lines never intersect.
