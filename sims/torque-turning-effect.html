Why is a force applied at 90° to the arm the most effective at turning? What happens to the torque when the force points straight along the arm, and why?

Conclusion

To undo a tight bolt you push the spanner at right angles and as far from the bolt as you can. Push along the spanner instead and nothing turns. Torque captures exactly why.

Torque (the moment of a force)

The torque (or moment) of a force about a pivot measures its turning effect. It depends on the force, the distance from the pivot, and the angle between them:

τ=rFsin θ
τ is torque (N·m), F the force (N), r the distance from pivot to where the force acts (m), and θ the angle between the arm and the force.

Splitting the force: perpendicular vs along the arm

Any force applied to the arm can be split into two components at right angles to each other:

⟂

Perpendicular component, F sinθ

This is the part at right angles to the arm. It is the only part that produces a torque: τ = (F sinθ) × r.

∥

Component along the arm, F cosθ

This part points along the arm, straight through (or away from) the pivot. Its line of action passes through the pivot, so it has zero moment arm and produces no turning effect.

Two ways to get the same torque

You can group the equation τ = r F sinθ in two equally valid ways:

τ=(Fsin θ)×r
Perpendicular component of force × distance.
τ=F×(rsin θ)
Force × the moment arm d = r sinθ (the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action).

What the angle does

90°

Maximum turning effect

At θ = 90° the whole force is perpendicular (sin 90° = 1), so τ = rF — the largest torque for that force and distance.

0°/180°

No turning effect

When the force points straight along the arm (θ = 0° or 180°), sinθ = 0, so τ = 0. The force acts through the pivot and cannot turn it.

Everyday rule. For the biggest turning effect: push at 90° to the arm, and push as far from the pivot as possible. That is why door handles sit at the outer edge and why a longer spanner loosens a stubborn nut.
Q1 · Define

Write the equation for the torque of a force and define each symbol, including the angle θ.

τ = r F sinθ, where τ is the torque (N·m), F the applied force (N), r the distance from the pivot to the point where the force acts (m), and θ the angle between the arm (the line from pivot to that point) and the force.
Q2 · Components

A force F acts on an arm at angle θ. Write the components perpendicular to and along the arm, and state which produces the torque.

Perpendicular component = F sinθ; component along the arm = F cosθ. Only the perpendicular component (F sinθ) produces a torque; the along-arm component acts through the pivot and contributes nothing.
Q3 · Calculate

A 25 N force is applied to a spanner 0.20 m from the bolt, at 90° to the spanner. Find the torque.

τ = r F sinθ = 0.20 × 25 × sin90° = 0.20 × 25 × 1 = 5.0 N·m.
Q4 · Angle

The same 25 N force is now applied at 30° to the spanner. Find the new torque, and say what fraction of the maximum it is.

τ = 0.20 × 25 × sin30° = 0.20 × 25 × 0.5 = 2.5 N·m. That is half the maximum (5.0 N·m), because sin30° = 0.5.
Q5 · Moment arm

Define the moment arm and explain how τ = F × d relates to τ = r F sinθ.

The moment arm d is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force, d = r sinθ. Substituting gives τ = F × (r sinθ) = r F sinθ, so the two forms are identical.
Q6 · Explain

Why does pushing a door near its hinge, or pushing it at a shallow angle, make it hard to open?

Pushing near the hinge makes r small, so τ = rF sinθ is small. Pushing at a shallow angle makes θ small, so sinθ is small and again the torque is small. The biggest torque comes from pushing far from the hinge (large r) and at 90° (sinθ = 1) — try both in the simulator.
