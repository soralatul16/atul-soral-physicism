1.3Mass and weight

How much matter is in an object, and how hard gravity pulls on it. Squash, heat and cut a lump of clay to see what changes its mass, load a newton meter on different planets, follow an astronaut’s suitcase from Earth to the Moon and beyond, and balance mystery objects on a beam balance. Choose Core or Extended at the top.

Notes

Everything in this topic, in the order of the syllabus. Supplement sections are marked S.

Mass

The quantity of matter in an object — and what does (and does not) change it.

What the syllabus saysCoreState that mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer

Weight

The gravitational force on a mass, measured in newtons with a newton meter.

What the syllabus saysCoreState that weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass CoreRecall and use the equation g = W ÷ m (so W = m × g)

Gravitational field strength

Force per unit mass — and why it is the same number as the acceleration of free fall.

What the syllabus saysCoreDefine gravitational field strength as force per unit mass; recall and use the equation g = W ÷ m and know that this is equivalent to the acceleration of free fall

Balances

Comparing weights — and so masses — with a beam balance.

What the syllabus saysCoreKnow that weights (and masses) may be compared using a balance

Gravitational fields S

Weight is what a gravitational field does to a mass.

What the syllabus saysSupplement SDescribe, and use the concept of, weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass
