01Energy resources

An energy resource is a store of energy that we can use, either directly (for heating or transport) or to generate electricity.

Renewable vs non-renewableRenewable resources are replaced naturally as fast as we use them, so they will not run out: biofuels, hydroelectric, waves, tides, geothermal, solar and wind.
Non-renewable resources are used up faster than they are replaced, so they will eventually run out: fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) and nuclear fuel (uranium).

02Boiler, turbine and generator

Most power stations generate electricity in the same three stages. Only the heat source changes.

  1. Boiler: the heat source boils water to make high-pressure steam.
  2. Turbine: the steam pushes on the turbine blades and makes them spin.
  3. Generator: the turbine turns the generator, which produces electricity.
Which resources use which parts?Boiler + turbine + generator: fossil fuels, biofuels, nuclear, geothermal.
Turbine + generator only (moving air or water turns the turbine directly): hydroelectric, tides, waves, wind.
None of these: solar cells make electricity directly from light; solar panels heat water directly.

03How each resource works

ResourceHow useful energy or electricity is obtained
Fossil fuels
coal, oil, gas		Burned to release the chemical store. The heat boils water in a boiler; the steam drives a turbine and generator. Oil is also refined into petrol and diesel for transport.
Biofuels
wood, biogas, bioethanol		Made from plants or animal waste. Burned like fossil fuels (boiler → turbine → generator) or used as vehicle fuel.
HydroelectricWater stored behind a dam has a GPE store. It flows down through pipes, turning turbines and generators.
TidesA barrage (dam) across an estuary traps water at high tide. As the tide falls, water flows out through turbines that drive generators.
WavesThe up-and-down motion of waves moves floating machines or pushes air through a turbine, which drives a generator.
GeothermalHot rocks deep underground heat water pumped down to them. The steam returns to drive a turbine and generator, or the hot water heats buildings.
Nuclear fuelFission of uranium releases energy from the nuclear store. It heats water in a boiler (via a heat exchanger); steam drives a turbine and generator.
Solar cellsLight from the Sun is absorbed by a solar (photovoltaic) cell, which produces electricity directly. No turbine or generator.
Solar panelsInfrared radiation from the Sun heats water flowing through black pipes, giving hot water for homes. No electricity is generated.
WindThe Sun heats the Earth unevenly, causing winds. Moving air turns the blades of a wind turbine, which drives a generator.

CriterionThe question to ask
RenewabilityWill it run out?
AvailabilityIs it available everywhere, or only in some places (e.g. only near volcanoes, coasts or rivers)?
ReliabilityCan it produce electricity whenever we need it, or does it depend on the weather or time of day?
ScaleHow much electricity can one station produce? Could it power a city, or just a house?

05The Sun is the main source of our energy

Radiation from the Sun is the original source of energy for almost all our energy resources:

  • Fossil fuels and biofuels: plants captured sunlight by photosynthesis (millions of years ago for fossil fuels).
  • Wind: the Sun heats the Earth’s surface unevenly, creating convection currents in the air.
  • Waves: caused by wind, which comes from the Sun.
  • Hydroelectric: the Sun evaporates water, which falls as rain on high ground.
  • Solar cells and solar panels: use the Sun’s light and infrared directly.
The three exceptionsGeothermal (heat from radioactive decay inside the Earth), nuclear (energy in uranium nuclei) and tidal (caused mainly by the Moon’s gravity) do NOT come from the Sun.

06Nuclear fusion in the Sun

The Sun releases energy by nuclear fusion: in its core, hydrogen nuclei join together to form helium nuclei. This releases a huge amount of energy, which leaves the Sun as electromagnetic radiation.

Fusion power on EarthResearch is being carried out (for example, the ITER project in France) to find out how energy released by nuclear fusion can be used to produce electrical energy on a large scale. Fusion needs extremely high temperatures (over 100 million °C) and is very hard to control, so it is not yet used in power stations. Its fuel (hydrogen isotopes) is plentiful and it produces very little radioactive waste.

07Efficiency

No device transfers all its input energy usefully. Some is always wasted, usually to the thermal store of the surroundings. Efficiency tells us what fraction of the input energy is transferred usefully.

efficiency = frac{useful energy output}{total energy input} × 100%
efficiency = frac{useful power output}{total power input} × 100%
  • Efficiency has no unit. It can be a decimal (0.35) or a percentage (35%).
  • It can never be more than 100%, because energy cannot be created.
  • A more efficient device wastes less energy, so it costs less to run and needs less fuel.
Rearranged forms
useful output = efficiency × total input
total input = frac{useful output}{efficiency}
Typical efficienciesCoal power station ≈ 35% · Gas power station ≈ 50% · Nuclear ≈ 33% · Hydroelectric ≈ 90% · Wind turbine ≈ 45% · Solar cell ≈ 20% · LED ≈ 40% · Filament lamp ≈ 10% · Electric motor ≈ 80%

08Worked examples

Example 8.1A coal power station receives 2000 MJ of chemical energy and generates 700 MJ of electrical energy. Calculate its efficiency.
efficiency = frac{useful energy output}{total energy input} × 100% = frac{700}{2000} × 100% => 35%
Example 8.2A solar cell receives light at a rate of 150 W and produces 30 W of electrical power. Calculate its efficiency.
efficiency = frac{30}{150} × 100% => 20%
Example 8.3A wind turbine is 40% efficient. The wind delivers 5.0 MW of power to it. Calculate the electrical power output.
useful power = efficiency × total power input = 0.40 × 5.0 MW => 2.0 MW
Example 8.4A motor has an efficiency of 75%. It lifts a load, giving it 450 J of GPE. Calculate the electrical energy supplied to the motor.
total input = frac{useful output}{efficiency} = frac{450}{0.75} => 600 J
Example 8.5A power station has three stages: boiler 90% efficient, turbine 45% efficient, generator 95% efficient. 1000 MJ of fuel energy goes in. Determine the electrical output and the overall efficiency.
# Boiler: thermal store of steam = 0.90 × 1000 = 900 MJ # Turbine: kinetic store = 0.45 × 900 = 405 MJ # Generator: electrical output = 0.95 × 405 => 385 MJ # Overall: efficiency = frac{385}{1000} × 100% => 38.5%

09Exam technique

Command termIn this topic
Describe“Describe how electricity is generated in a coal power station.” → Name the stages: fuel burned → boiler → steam → turbine → generator.
State“State one advantage of tidal power.” → One short point: “It is renewable.”
Explain“Explain why wind power is unreliable.” → Give the reason: “It depends on the wind, which does not always blow.”
Suggest“Suggest a suitable energy resource for a remote island.” → Apply ideas: wind or waves, with a reason.
CalculateUse the efficiency equation. Show the equation, substitution and answer as a % or decimal.
Common mistakesSaying solar cells use a turbine (they do not). Saying tidal energy comes from the Sun (it comes mainly from the Moon). Giving an efficiency greater than 100%. Writing “energy is lost” without saying where it goes (the thermal store of the surroundings).
