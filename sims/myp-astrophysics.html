Astrophysics

How far away are the stars, why does the Moon change shape, what keeps a satellite up, how can we tell what a star is made of without going there — and how do we know the universe had a beginning? Every tab has a story, a hands-on lab and a mission. ★ Extension boxes go further for MYP 5.

Key concept: Systems  ·  Related concepts: evidence, models, energy, movement, patterns  ·  Global context: Orientation in space and time
Statement of inquiry: By interpreting the evidence carried to us by light and gravity, scientists build models of systems — from the Solar System to the whole universe — that explain where we came from.

Notes

All the physics of this unit, with diagrams and worked examples. Use them alongside the labs.

Scale of the universe

From the Moon to the edge of the observable universe: distances, light-years and looking back in time.

Earth, Moon and Sun

Day and night, seasons, phases of the Moon, eclipses and tides — one system of three bodies.

Gravity and orbits

Why things orbit, how fast satellites go, and Kepler’s pattern for the planets.

Light and spectra

Light is our only messenger from the stars: its colour tells us temperature, its dark lines tell us what stars are made of.

Redshift and Hubble’s law

Moving sources stretch or squash their waves — and almost every galaxy is moving away from us.

Stars and the HR diagram

Nuclear fusion powers the stars; plotting brightness against temperature reveals their families.

Life cycle of stars

A star’s mass decides its whole life story — and how it ends.

Exoplanets

Finding planets around other stars from a tiny dip in starlight — and asking whether any could host life.

The Big Bang

The evidence that the universe began hot and dense about 13.8 billion years ago, and has been expanding ever since.
